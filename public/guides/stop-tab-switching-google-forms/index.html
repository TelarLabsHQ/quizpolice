How to Stop Students from Switching Tabs in Google Forms

Google Forms can’t tell when a student opens another tab, except in one special case. Here is what each option really does, and how to choose.

Students switch tabs during online quizzes for the same reason they used to glance at a neighbor’s paper: it’s easy and nobody sees it. A search engine, a group chat or an AI chatbot is one tap away. The good news is that you have several ways to deal with it, from settings that block it on some devices to tools that make it visible on every device.

Can Google Forms detect tab switching?

No. A normal Google Form doesn’t know what else is open on the student’s device. It only receives the answers when the student presses Submit. There is no setting that warns you when a student leaves the form and comes back.

The one exception is locked mode, described below. It doesn’t detect switching; it prevents it, and only on certain devices.

Option 1: Locked mode (school Chromebooks only)

Locked mode keeps students inside the quiz: they can’t open other tabs or apps until they submit. To use it, all of these must be true:

  • You and your students use a Google Workspace for Education account from your school.
  • Students take the quiz on Chromebooks managed by the school.
  • The form is set up as a quiz.

To turn it on, open the form, go to Settings, open the Quizzes section, and turn on locked mode. If you don’t see the option, your account or your school’s settings don’t support it.

Locked mode doesn’t work on phones, tablets, Windows laptops or Macs. In many classrooms, especially where students use their own phones, that rules it out.

Option 2: Make tab switching visible on any device

If students use phones or a mix of devices, you can’t block other apps, but you can make leaving the quiz visible. That’s what QuizPolice does:

  1. Create your quiz in Google Forms as usual.
  2. Open the QuizPolice teacher dashboard, give the quiz a name, and paste the form link. Press Check this link.
  3. Choose how many exits are allowed before a student is locked, for example 3.
  4. Show the 6-digit code or QR code on the board. Students type their name and answer the form inside QuizPolice.
  5. Your dashboard shows, live, who is answering, who just left the quiz, for how long, and who is locked.

Before they start, students see that leaving is recorded. In practice, that warning does most of the work: most students simply stay.

Two settings in the form matter for this to work: turn off Restrict to users in your organization, and don’t use Collect email addresses: Verified. Both require a sign-in that browsers often block when the form is shown inside another site. The supported quizzes page explains why.

Option 3: Make switching less useful

Even with no monitoring at all, you can make a quick search much less helpful:

  • Shuffle question order and shuffle option order, so answers can’t be passed around as “number 4 is B”.
  • Release grades later, after everyone has finished, so early finishers can’t share the answer key.
  • Write questions that apply ideas to a new situation, a local example or a graph you made. These are much harder to search than definitions.
  • Use a time limit. Searching every question takes time. QuizPolice can add a timer or schedule the quiz so everyone starts and ends together.

Which option should you use?

Your classroomBest choice
School-managed Chromebooks and school Google accountsLocked mode, plus good question design
Students’ own phones, tablets or laptopsQuizPolice to see and limit exits, plus shuffling and a time limit
A mix of devicesQuizPolice for everyone, so all students follow the same rules
Homework quizzes taken at homeAn open period in QuizPolice with a time limit, and lower stakes for the quiz

What no tool can stop

No software on one device can see a second phone under the desk, a friend sitting nearby, or notes on paper. Walk around the room, ask for phones not in use to be put away, and talk about honesty before the quiz. A record of exits is a reason to ask a student what happened, not proof that they cheated.

Quick checklist

  1. Decide: locked mode (school Chromebooks) or QuizPolice (any device).
  2. Turn on shuffling and release grades later.
  3. Turn off sign-in requirements if you use QuizPolice.
  4. Set a fair time limit.
  5. Tell students what is recorded, and supervise the room.
