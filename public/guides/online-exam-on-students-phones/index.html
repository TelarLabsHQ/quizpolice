How to Give an Online Exam on Students’ Phones

In many schools, students’ phones are the only devices every class has. Phones can work well for online exams, as long as you plan for small screens, weak signals and batteries.

Before exam day

Write the exam for a small screen

  • Keep questions short. One idea per question. Long passages are better on paper or shown on the board.
  • Avoid big tables and grids. Matching grids and wide tables are hard to read and tap on a phone. Split them into separate multiple-choice questions.
  • Make pictures large and clear. Crop diagrams tightly and avoid tiny labels.
  • Use sections. Splitting a long exam into a few pages makes it less tiring to scroll, and students lose less if something goes wrong.

Check the settings

  • Don’t require sign-in if students will take it inside a monitor like QuizPolice. See supported quizzes for the exact settings.
  • Shuffle questions and options, and release scores after everyone has finished.
  • Try the exam yourself on a low-cost phone, not only on your laptop.

Tell students what to prepare

  • A charged phone, at least 50%, and a charger if there are outlets in the room.
  • Enough mobile data or a working Wi-Fi connection. A form-based quiz uses little data, but pictures and videos use more.
  • Other apps closed and notifications silenced. On most phones, Do Not Disturb or Focus mode does this.

Set up the exam in QuizPolice

  1. On the teacher dashboard, name the exam and paste the quiz link. Press Check this link.
  2. Choose Scheduled exam, set the start time and length. Students who open the link early wait on a countdown, and everyone starts and ends together.
  3. Choose how many exits are allowed before a student is locked. Three is a fair start for phones, because a notification or a call can pull a student out by accident.
  4. Choose what happens when time runs out. The default gives students 1 more minute to press Submit, and marks late submissions on your dashboard.

On exam day

  • Show the code and QR on the board. Students scan or type the 6-digit code, then their name and section.
  • Seat students with space between them if the room allows, and ask for bags and second phones to be put away.
  • Keep your dashboard open on your laptop or phone. You see who is answering, who left the quiz, who is locked and who has submitted.
  • Walk around. The dashboard shows you where to look; your presence does the rest.

When something goes wrong

ProblemWhat to do
A student’s phone died or the browser closed.They join again with the same code and name. They may appear twice on your dashboard, the old entry and the new one, which also shows you what happened. Answers that were never submitted on the form are usually lost, so give extra time if needed with +5 minutes.
A student was locked after a call or notification.Ask what happened, then press Unlock on your dashboard if you’re satisfied.
A student’s data or Wi-Fi ran out.Let them share a classmate’s hotspot after that classmate has submitted, or use a school device.
Some students were absent or couldn’t finish.Use Open make-up quiz on the dashboard for a later time with its own timing.
A student has no phone.Lend a school device, pair them with a student who finished, or give a paper copy of the same questions.

After the exam

  • Turn off Accepting responses on the form.
  • Check the official answers in the form’s own Responses. QuizPolice doesn’t see answers; it records joins, exits, locks and submissions.
  • Download the QuizPolice results as a CSV for your records.
  • Talk privately with students whose records raise questions. An exit is a reason to ask, not proof.

Making it fair for everyone

Not every student has the same phone or the same data plan. Keep a few school devices or paper copies ready, avoid questions that need fast internet, and don’t let a technical problem lower a student’s score. When students see that the exam is fair to everyone, they are also less tempted to cheat.
