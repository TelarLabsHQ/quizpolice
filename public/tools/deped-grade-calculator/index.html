How grades are computed under DO 015, s. 2026

Starting School Year 2026–2027, public schools follow DepEd Order No. 015, s. 2026, which replaces DO 8, s. 2015. The school year has three terms, only summative work counts toward grades (daily formative quizzes don’t), and transmutation is being phased out.

  1. Percentage score for each component = total raw score ÷ total highest possible score × 100.
  2. Exams (for most subjects) combine Summative Test 1 (30%), Summative Test 2 (30%) and the Term Exam (40%).
  3. Weighted score = percentage score × the component’s weight.
  4. Initial grade = the sum of the weighted scores.
  5. Term grade = the initial grade transmuted with the SY 2026–2027 adjusted table, rounded to a whole number. From SY 2027–2028, Grades 4 to 12 use the initial grade itself, with no transmutation.
  6. Final grade = the average of the three term grades, rounded. The general average is the average of all final grades.

Example

A Grade 7 Math student has 82% in Written Work, 80% in Performance Tasks and 85.6% in Exams. With weights of 20%, 50% and 30%: 16.40 + 40.00 + 25.68 = an initial grade of 82.08. On the SY 2026–2027 table, 81.80–82.97 becomes 85, which is “Benchmarking.”

Questions

Is this the official DepEd computation?

It follows DepEd Order No. 015, s. 2026 as published, including the SY 2026–2027 adjusted transmutation table. Small differences can come from how your school rounds percentage scores. Always confirm with your school’s official e-Class Record.

Can I still use the old table?

Yes. Choose “Old table (DO 8, s. 2015)” and the old weights, for example for Grade 12 learners who are not yet on the strengthened SHS curriculum, or for schools that still use it.

Are my scores saved?

Only in your own browser, so they’re still here when you come back. Nothing is sent to QuizPolice.

Sources: DepEd Order No. 015, s. 2026 (Revised Classroom Assessment and Grading Guidelines) and DepEd Order No. 8, s. 2015.
