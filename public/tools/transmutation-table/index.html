Transmutation table maker

Showing a shared transmutation rule.

Scores between are spread evenly: one band for each whole grade above the passing grade, and one for each below it.

Grades below passing are shown in red. Copy table pastes neatly into Excel or Google Sheets.

What is a transmutation table?

A transmutation table turns a raw percentage into a grade on your school’s scale. Many schools don’t want 60% of the items correct to show up as a “60”, so the passing score is set to a passing grade, like 60% becoming 75, and the scores above and below are spread out from there.

How to make your own

  1. Type your passing score as a percentage, and the grade it should become.
  2. Type the grades for a score of 0% and 100%. The lowest grade is often 60 or 65, so no one gets a zero for trying.
  3. Read the full table, or choose For a test with… and type the number of items to get every raw score with its grade, ready to use while checking papers.
  4. Print it, copy it into a spreadsheet, send the link to colleagues, or use it straight away in the grade calculator.

Example

With a passing score of 60% becoming 75, 0% becoming 60 and 100% becoming 100, the 25 grades from 75 to 100 share the scores from 60% to 100%, so each band is 1.60 points wide. Below passing, the 15 grades from 60 to 74 share 0% to 60%, 4 points each. That’s the same rule as the K to 12 table from 2015.

The K to 12 tables

Both K to 12 tables are ready to use above. In the 2026–2027 adjusted table, an initial grade of 70 is needed for a passing 75, compared with 60 in the 2015 table. From school year 2027–2028, Grades 4 to 12 no longer transmute: an initial grade of 75 is a final 75.

Questions

Is this an official table?

The tool is independent and not affiliated with any school system or agency. The two K to 12 tables follow the published tables; always confirm with your school’s official records.

Is my table saved?

Your rule is saved in this browser only. The link to your rule carries just the four numbers, so anyone who opens it sees the same table.
