    How to play

    1. Choose a question type. Name the capital shows a country and asks for its capital. Name the country works the other way around.
    2. Choose a region, or World for questions from everywhere.
    3. Press Start and pick one of four answers. If you get it wrong, you’ll see the right answer straight away.
    4. After 10 questions you’ll see your score and a list of the ones to remember.

    On a computer you can also press the keys 1 to 4 to answer and Enter for the next question.

    What’s included

    The game includes about 150 countries from Asia, Europe, Africa, the Americas and Oceania. The wrong answers always come from the same region as the right one, so you can’t guess just by knowing the continent.

    A few countries are left out on purpose because their capital is complicated: some have more than one official capital (like South Africa and Bolivia), and some are moving their government to a new city. Those make great questions for a class discussion, but not for a quick quiz.

    Tips for remembering capitals

    • Practice one region at a time. Master Asia, then add Europe. It’s easier than learning the whole world at once.
    • Play both ways. Knowing that Canberra is the capital of Australia is different from being asked which country Canberra belongs to. Doing both makes the memory stronger.
    • Look for clues in the names. Kuwait City, Mexico City, Guatemala City and Panama City share their country’s name. Others are famous traps: the capital of Australia is Canberra, not Sydney, and the capital of Canada is Ottawa, not Toronto.
    • Make a story. Linking a capital to a picture or a silly sentence helps it stick.
    • Come back tomorrow. Replaying the ones you missed after a day or two is one of the most effective ways to remember them.

    For teachers and parents

    Geography Practice is free and works on phones, tablets and computers, with no sign-up. Use one round as a lesson warm-up, set “score 10/10 in your region” as homework, or let students compete for the best score in class. Scores stay on the device. Nothing is sent to us.

    For more ways to help students remember, see How to Study for a Quiz: 7 Tips That Actually Work. When it’s time for a real quiz online, QuizPolice helps teachers keep it fair.

    I think an answer is wrong. Please tell us. Capitals sometimes change, and we’ll update the list.
