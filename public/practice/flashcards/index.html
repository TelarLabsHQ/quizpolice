How to make flash cards

  1. Make a deck. Type a term on the front and its meaning on the back. Or open Paste a list and paste two columns from Excel or Google Sheets, or lines like “Hola - Hello”.
  2. Study. Tap a card to flip it, then mark it Got it or Still learning. At the end of a round, study only the cards you’re still learning.
  3. Quiz yourself. Ten multiple-choice questions from your own cards, with a review of the ones you missed.
  4. Print cut-out cards, or share the deck with a link.

Tips for studying with flash cards

  • Say the answer before you flip. Trying to remember, even when you get it wrong, makes the memory stronger than just reading.
  • Keep cards short: one idea per card. Split long answers into two cards.
  • Study a little every day. Ten minutes a day for a week works better than an hour the night before.
  • Mix it up. Turn on Shuffle, and try Show the back first to practice in the other direction, like English to Spanish.
  • Focus on the hard ones. After each round, study the still-learning cards until they’re all “got it”.

For teachers

  • Share a deck with your class: make it once, tap Share this deck, and post the link in your class group or learning platform. Each student gets their own copy to study.
  • Paste from your lesson plan: copy a vocabulary table from a document or spreadsheet into Paste a list.
  • Print for the classroom: fold cards work on any printer. For card stock, use double-sided printing.
  • Before a test, students can also warm up with the practice games.

Questions

Do I need an account?

No. Your decks are saved in this browser on this device. To move a deck to another phone or computer, open its share link there.

How does the share link work?

The whole deck is packed into the link itself, so nothing is uploaded to our servers. Anyone who opens it gets their own copy, and their changes don’t affect yours.

Can I add pictures?

Not yet. Cards can have text, line breaks and emoji.

How many cards can a deck have?

As many as you like on your device. For sharing, decks of up to about 200 short cards make links that work in most apps.
