    How to play

    1. Choose a question type. Which came first? shows two events and you tap the earlier one. Pick the year shows one event and four possible years.
    2. Choose an era, or All eras for the full sweep of history.
    3. After each answer you’ll see the dates, so every question teaches something.
    4. At the end, review the events you missed, in time order.

    Why it helps to know when things happened

    History makes much more sense when you can place events on a timeline. Knowing that the printing press came before the Reformation, or that Haiti became independent before most of South America, helps you see cause and effect, and how events in different parts of the world were connected. You don’t need to memorize every date: a few strong “anchor dates” make everything else easier to place.

    Tips for remembering dates

    • Learn anchor dates first. Pick one event per century that you know well, and place other events before or after it.
    • Know how centuries work. The 1400s are the 15th century, and the 1900s are the 20th century. The century number is always one more than the first digits.
    • BCE counts backwards. 776 BCE is earlier than 323 BCE. The bigger the BCE number, the longer ago it was.
    • Connect events across the world. Zheng He’s voyages (1405) came almost a century before Columbus (1492) and Vasco da Gama (1498).
    • Practice both ways. “Which came first?” builds a sense of order. “Pick the year” builds precise recall.

    The timeline

    Every event in the game, in order. Dates marked “about” are approximate, because historians estimate them from the evidence available.

    Ancient (up to 500 CE)

    • about 2560 BCE: The Great Pyramid of Giza is completed in Egypt
    • about 1754 BCE: Hammurabi’s Code of laws is written in Babylon
    • 776 BCE: The first recorded Olympic Games are held in Greece
    • 323 BCE: Alexander the Great dies in Babylon
    • 221 BCE: Qin Shi Huang unites China and becomes its first emperor
    • 44 BCE: Julius Caesar is killed in Rome
    • 79 CE: Mount Vesuvius erupts and buries Pompeii
    • 80 CE: The Colosseum opens in Rome
    • about 105 CE: Cai Lun improves papermaking in Han China
    • 476 CE: The Western Roman Empire falls

    Middle Ages (500 to 1500)

    • 622 CE: The Prophet Muhammad migrates from Mecca to Medina (the Hijra)
    • 800 CE: Charlemagne is crowned emperor in Rome
    • 1206: Genghis Khan founds the Mongol Empire
    • 1215: King John of England agrees to the Magna Carta
    • 1324: Mansa Musa of Mali makes his famous pilgrimage to Mecca
    • 1347: The Black Death reaches Europe
    • 1368: The Ming dynasty begins in China
    • 1405: Zheng He sets out on the first of his great treasure voyages
    • 1453: Constantinople falls to the Ottoman Empire
    • about 1455: Gutenberg prints his Bible with movable metal type
    • 1492: Christopher Columbus reaches the Americas
    • 1498: Vasco da Gama reaches India by sea

    Early modern (1500 to 1800)

    • 1517: Martin Luther posts his Ninety-five Theses
    • 1521: Magellan’s expedition reaches the Philippines
    • 1522: The first voyage around the world is completed
    • 1603: The Tokugawa shogunate begins in Japan
    • 1610: Galileo sees the moons of Jupiter through his telescope
    • 1616: William Shakespeare dies
    • 1620: The Mayflower arrives in North America
    • 1644: The Qing dynasty takes Beijing
    • 1687: Isaac Newton publishes his laws of motion and gravity
    • 1770: James Cook reaches the east coast of Australia
    • 1776: The United States declares independence
    • 1789: The French Revolution begins with the storming of the Bastille

    Modern (1800 to today)

    • 1804: Haiti becomes independent
    • 1815: Napoleon is defeated at Waterloo
    • 1822: Brazil declares independence from Portugal
    • 1825: The first public steam railway opens in England
    • 1859: Charles Darwin publishes On the Origin of Species
    • 1865: The American Civil War ends
    • 1869: The Suez Canal opens
    • 1876: Alexander Graham Bell patents the telephone
    • 1893: New Zealand becomes the first country where women can vote in national elections
    • 1898: The Philippines declares independence from Spain
    • 1903: The Wright brothers make the first powered airplane flight
    • 1914: World War I begins
    • 1917: The Russian Revolution takes place
    • 1928: Alexander Fleming discovers penicillin
    • 1945: World War II ends and the United Nations is founded
    • 1947: India and Pakistan become independent
    • 1949: The People’s Republic of China is founded
    • 1957: Ghana becomes independent
    • 1961: Yuri Gagarin becomes the first person in space
    • 1969: Apollo 11 astronauts walk on the Moon
    • 1989: The Berlin Wall falls
    • 1991: The World Wide Web becomes available to the public
    • 1994: Nelson Mandela becomes president of South Africa
    • 2002: Euro coins and banknotes come into use
    • 2003: The Human Genome Project is completed

    For teachers

    Use a round as a lesson starter, ask students to explain why one event came before another, or have them add an event from your own country’s history to the timeline. The game is free, needs no sign-up and works on phones. Scores stay on the student’s device.
