    How to play

    1. Choose a level, or Mix it up for a bit of everything.
    2. Press Start. You have 60 seconds.
    3. Tap the right answer. A wrong answer shows the correct one for a moment, then the game moves on.
    4. At the end, review the ones you missed.

    The levels

    • Percent of a number: 15% of 240, 75% of 60, and so on.
    • Discounts & tips: sale prices and tips, the percent math you actually use when you shop or eat out.
    • Fractions, decimals, percents: 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%. These come up constantly in statistics, science and finance.
    • Estimation: pick the closest answer without calculating exactly, like 49 × 31 or the total of three prices.

    Shortcuts that make percents easy

    • 10% is just moving the decimal point. 10% of 240 is 24. Then 5% is half of that (12), and 15% is 24 + 12 = 36.
    • Percents can be flipped. 8% of 50 is the same as 50% of 8, which is 4.
    • 25% is a quarter, 50% is a half, 75% is three quarters. 75% of 60 = 60 − 15 = 45.
    • Sale price = price × what’s left. 30% off means you pay 70%: 0.7 × $80 = $56.
    • Know the eighths. 1/8 = 12.5%, so 3/8 = 37.5%, 5/8 = 62.5% and 7/8 = 87.5%.

    Why estimation is a real skill

    Estimating is how you catch mistakes. If a calculator says 49 × 31 = 15,190, a quick estimate (50 × 30 = 1,500) tells you a digit slipped. Good estimators round to easy numbers, calculate, and then ask whether the answer makes sense. It’s useful in exams, in lab work, in budgeting and when checking a bill.

    • Round each number to one or two easy digits before multiplying.
    • For a total of prices, round each price to the nearest dollar.
    • For “about 19% of 503”, think “20% of 500”, which is 100.

    Who it’s for

    High school and college students preparing for exams in statistics, business, economics, nursing or science, and anyone who wants faster mental math for daily life. Teachers can use one round as a warm-up. It’s free, there’s no sign-up, and scores stay on your device.
